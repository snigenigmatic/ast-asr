03 · Publication

ACL SRW 2026 — submitted.

Fairness Blind Spots in Transformer-Based Indian English ASR

A structured audit of why the Indian-English performance gap has persisted. We review 23 papers from 2019 to 2026 across the ACL Anthology, IEEE Xplore, and arXiv, and document a measurement absence: across all surveyed work, no study reports a demographic parity gap, an equal opportunity gap, or a per-group noise robustness score for Indian speakers.

Contribution

A minimal evaluation protocol (ΔDP, ΔEO, Δgnoise) with a Poisson drop-in-deviance significance test, computable on existing Indian-English corpora without additional data collection.

Why this paper, why now

The infrastructure exists — Svarah (AI4Bharat), SPIRE-SIES, AccentDB, RESPIN-S1.0 — and Rai et al. (2025) have shown a fairness leaderboard is tractable on US speech. None of it has been applied to Indian speakers.

ACL SRW 2026 23 papers reviewed 3 metrics + 1 test No new data needed

Authors: Kaustubh, A. Sharma, A. M. Udaya, A. V. Holla · Guide: B. J. Das. Department of CSE & CSE-AIML, PES University.

04 · Methodology

The evaluation protocol.

Three disaggregated metrics, one significance test. No new corpus required.

ΔDP

Demographic Parity Gap

ΔDP = maxi,j∈G |WER(gi) − WER(gj)|

Tests whether transcription accuracy is independent of accent-group membership. Any value above 5 pp is flagged for explanation.

ΔEO

Equal Opportunity Gap

ΔEO = maxi,j∈G |TPR(gi) − TPR(gj)|

Word-level true-positive rates across groups. Complements ΔDP — a model can pass parity while failing equal opportunity.

Δgnoise

Per-Group Noise Robustness

Δnoise(gi) = WER(gi, 0 dB SNR) − WER(gi, clean)

Isolates whether noise degrades certain accent groups disproportionately. Evaluated using SPIRE-SIES naturalistic noise.

Significance: Poisson drop-in-deviance χ² test on word error counts, with utterance length as a covariate. p < 0.05 threshold for systematic disparity. Following Jahan (2025) and Rai et al. (2025).

05 · The original plan

What we proposed.

Adversarial debiasing via gradient reversal

A shared Wav2Vec2 encoder (with LoRA adapters on attention projections) is trained to minimise CTC loss while a gradient-reversal layer feeds an MLP adversary that tries to predict the speaker's language family. The reversed gradients push the encoder toward a representation from which demographic attributes cannot be decoded.

Ganin & Lempitsky 2015 Zhang et al. 2018 (AIES) SPIRE-SIES training
Loss

L = LCTC + λ · Ladv-rev

λ ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.30}, warmup over first 20% of steps. Theoretically sound; widely used in NLP and vision fairness work.

06 · The diagnosis

GRL widened the gap.

Across every λ we swept on Svarah-eval, the demographic parity gap moved in the wrong direction. The intervention backfired for exactly the group most in need of correction.

ft-w2v2 · LoRA, no GRL ΔDP 9.0 pp
Hybrid · λ = 0.05 ΔDP 10.7 pp
Hybrid · λ = 0.10 ΔDP 12.5 pp
Hybrid · λ = 0.30 ΔDP 9.7 pp
Worst affected group Sino-Tibetan

500-sample Svarah eval · all GRL runs degrade ΔDP relative to the LoRA-only baseline (ft-w2v2). WER stays in a tight 41–42% band.

Root cause

SPIRE-SIES — our adversarial-training corpus — contains no Sino-Tibetan speakers. The adversary was therefore trained to distinguish Indo-Aryan from Dravidian only.

When evaluated on Svarah, the reversed gradients pushed the encoder away from a representation that worked for ST speakers, because ST was effectively out-of-distribution from the adversary's perspective.

The general principle

Adversarial debiasing fails when the minority group is absent or severely underrepresented in training data. The adversary cannot form a meaningful demographic signal for that group — and the GRL then degrades the encoder for it.

A known but underdocumented failure mode. We have empirical evidence of it in our own ablation.

07 · The pivot

Why GRPO is principled here.

We replaced gradient-reversal adversarial training with Group Relative Policy Optimisation — RL post-training on a fine-tuned Whisper-small + LoRA policy.

01

Operates on outputs, not latents

GRL tries to make internal representations demographic-invariant. If the corpus lacks a group, no amount of gradient reversal can fix that. GRPO's fairness reward operates on decoded text — per-family WER on the actual evaluation distribution.

No latent contortion required
02

Optimises the metric directly

GRL uses demographic-classification accuracy as a proxy. ΔDP is never in the loss. GRPO puts a ΔDP-derived signal directly in the reward, scaling per-utterance transcript rewards by family need. Tighter coupling between what we optimise and what we measure.

Reward = the thing we care about
03

Controls representation via sampling

GRPO rollouts use a family-balanced batch sampler. Each batch carries every family in the curriculum stage, so the fairness reward computes meaningful per-family WER gaps in every step. Demographic balance is something we control, not something we hope a corpus provides.

Sampling, not supplication
Prior art
2024–25
[17]Radhakrishnan et al. — first GRPO-RLHF for LLM-ASR; −18.4 % rel WER, reduced hallucinations, OOD-noise robustness. [18]Li et al. Overall WER drops 1.97 pp while ΔDP stays within the 5 pp threshold — but the headline is the p-value. The baseline showed a real, systematic gap across families; the RL model does not.

Per-family WER: Dravidian 17.0 → 16.2 % · Indo-Aryan 18.8 → 16.3 % · Sino-Tibetan 19.1 → 18.8 %. Held-out Svarah-eval split, 1,998 utterances (121 Sino-Tibetan) · Poisson drop-in-deviance test with utterance length as covariate.

10 · What's still broken

Noise robustness for ST.

With ΔDP brought inside threshold and stripped of statistical systematicity, the dominant remaining failure mode is per-group noise robustness — Sino-Tibetan and Indo-Aryan both collapse under additive noise; Dravidian survives.

Implication for next phase

Clean-condition fairness was a reward-shaping problem; we solved it. Noise-condition fairness is a coverage problem — the RL signal cannot manufacture acoustic robustness for a family the training corpus underrepresents. That requires data: synthetic or real.

Empirical precedent: FunAudio-ASR[20] reports >30 % relative WER reduction in noisy environments (dinner, supermarket) when RL is added with a noise-aware reward — direct support for moving Δnoise into the GRPO reward in Stage 02. ASR-TRA[21] further shows test-time RL with audio-text semantic rewards reduces WER on accented + noisy speech specifically.

Per-group Δnoise · whisper-small-rl

Dravidian +6.6 pp
Indo-Aryan +15.3 pp
Sino-Tibetan +16.9 pp

Δnoise(g) = WER(g, white noise @ 10 dB SNR) − WER(g, clean), measured on the full Svarah-eval split. Sino-Tibetan exhibits the largest absolute degradation — and is the smallest training-coverage family. Closing it is the next problem.

11 · Next phase

IndicTTS & the 3-stage curriculum.

Synthesise Sino-Tibetan-accented English at scale, fold it into a noise-aware curriculum, and re-target Δgnoise as the primary metric.

Done
Stage 01 · Clean baseline

All families · family-balanced sampler

v5 GRPO run: 1,500 steps on Svarah-train with SvarahBatchSampler guaranteeing every batch contains all three families. Clean audio only. Yields the 16.4 % / 2.64 pp / p = 0.078 result.

Next
Stage 02 · Noise-aware RL

+ in-training noise augmentation

Re-target Δgnoise as the primary metric. Noise probability 0.3 at SNR 10–20 dB during rollout generation; reward computed on the noisy hypothesis so the policy learns acoustic invariance — not just clean transcription.

Next
Stage 03 · Synthetic ST

+ IndicTTS Sino-Tibetan

Generate ST-accented English from tuannguyenvananh/indictts-english + XTTS-v2 voice cloning on Svarah ST reference clips. ST oversampled 3× in the batch sampler; noise prob 0.5, SNR 5–15 dB.

3-stage scheduler (ast-asr/rl/curriculum.py) is implemented and unit-tested; v5 used Stage-01 only. Stages 02–03 plug into the same SvarahBatchSampler. Quality filter for synthetic clips: discard samples whose ft-w2v2 WER exceeds 0.7.

Design choices traced to prior art: error-heavy sample selection during RL is more effective than random sampling[22] — directly motivating the 3× ST oversampling in Stage 03. Label-free per-accent adaptation via group-policy optimisation on unlabelled speech[23] validates synthetic-ST audio as a usable RL substrate even when ground-truth transcripts are imperfect.
