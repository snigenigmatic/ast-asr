# SPIRE-SIES cross-corpus reference ladder — 2026-08-25

Extends [`result-20260824.md`](result-20260824.md) with the two reference arms it
said it lacked (`zero-shot`, `sft-epoch1`). Same corpus, split, manifest,
decoding and grouping unit, so all six arms are directly comparable.

**Headline.** The post-training pipeline generalizes strongly to a second corpus
it was never trained on, and its gains are concentrated on the worst-off
speakers: overall WER 29.89% -> 24.79% (**−5.10 pp**, 17% relative) and
worst-20%-speaker WER 85.28% -> 61.41% (**−23.87 pp**). But the explicit fairness
mechanism inside it — the `reference_kl_beta=0.04` penalty — contributes nothing
measurable, and the language-family axis it optimizes is the least informative
axis available on this corpus.

In short: **the equity gains are real and they transfer, but they are not coming
from the fairness objective.**

## Findings

### 1. The pipeline transfers, and it helps the worst-off most

Every trained arm beats the base model, and every RL arm beats its own SFT
starting point. Improvements are **larger for harder speakers than on average**:

| Endpoint | base -> SFT | base -> best RL | ratio to overall gain |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| overall WER | −3.79 pp | −5.10 pp | 1.0x |
| worst-20% speaker WER | −19.71 pp | **−23.87 pp** | **4.7x** |
| gender gap (Male vs best) | −4.94 pp | −4.77 pp | — |

The worst-20% speakers gain almost five times the average gain, and the gender
gap is roughly halved (11.46 -> ~6.5 pp). This is a genuine equity improvement,
measured on a second corpus with real speaker-disjoint identities.

Most of it arrives with SFT (−3.79 pp overall, −19.71 pp on the hard tail); the
RL stage adds a further −0.50 to −1.32 pp.

Note this **reverses** the in-domain direction: on Svarah, SFT *failed* its clean
regression constraint (22.08% -> 24.17%). The same checkpoint improves clean WER
by 3.79 pp out-of-domain. Fitting Svarah more closely cost in-domain clean
accuracy while producing a model that generalizes better.

### 2. The fairness penalty contributes nothing

Within each matched seed the `beta=0.04` arm is never better than its `beta=0`
control on overall WER, and `h5-beta004` is the **worst** RL arm on the ladder
(25.60%) while its own control is the **best** (24.79%). The paired
speaker-clustered bootstraps in [`result-20260824.md`](result-20260824.md) put
the worst-family deltas at +1.69 pp `[−1.17, +7.27]` (seed 2026) and −0.03 pp
`[−1.98, +1.72]` (seed 2027) — no benefit, with the seed-2026 evidence leaning
toward harm.

So the equity gains in Finding 1 are attributable to the pipeline
(supervised fine-tuning plus RL post-training), **not** to the fairness term.
This is the cross-corpus, real-speaker version of the earlier finding that the
family-need weight cancels under group-relative normalization and that the v5
improvement was more plausibly the balanced sampler.

### 3. Family is the least informative group axis here, at every stage

| Arm | family | gender | worst-20% spk above overall |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `zero-shot` | 1.70 pp | 11.46 pp | 55.39 pp |
| `sft-epoch1` | 0.95 pp | 6.52 pp | 39.47 pp |
| RL arms | 0.56–2.33 pp | 6.69–7.70 pp | 36.62–40.37 pp |

The ordering **speaker >> gender >> family** holds in the base model, so it is a
property of this corpus and task rather than something training induced. The
FR-CISPO objective optimizes worst *family*, the smallest of the three by roughly
an order of magnitude. The worst-family label is also unstable, flipping between
Indo-Aryan and Dravidian across arms.

This does not contradict Svarah, where family gaps were large (41 pp zero-shot).
It shows family-gap prominence is **corpus dependent**, and argues for choosing
the grouping unit from evidence rather than convention.

## What this does not license

1. **The SFT -> RL gain is not attributed to the RL objective specifically.**
   There is no continued-supervised-training control matched for optimizer steps,
   so "more optimization on Svarah" is an unexcluded explanation for the
   −0.50 to −1.32 pp. Both beta arms improve similarly, which is consistent with
   a generic RL-stage effect rather than the penalty.
2. **No bootstrap intervals on the ladder comparisons.** The registered paired
   bootstrap covers the matched beta pairs. The base/SFT/RL contrasts here are
   point estimates on a fixed 198-speaker set; they are large (−3.79, −5.10,
   −23.87 pp) but not yet interval-estimated.
3. **Clean speech only.** No noise conditions; no robustness claim.
4. **Two families, no Sino-Tibetan, no age axis**, 13 of 17 shipped languages in
   the validation split.
5. **One generalization corpus**, clean read speech, so "transfers" means
   "transfers to SPIRE-SIES", not "transfers generally".
6. **Checkpoint provenance is still profile-cluster.** These adapters were
   trained and selected under Svarah's 115 demographic profile clusters. The
   *evaluation* grouping is a real speaker; the *training* grouping was not.

## Suggested next experiment

The single highest-value addition is a **continued-SFT control** matched to the
RL arms on optimizer steps, evaluated on this same split. That would separate
"the RL objective helped" from "more optimization helped" and close limitation 1,
which is currently the weakest joint in the positive half of the story.
