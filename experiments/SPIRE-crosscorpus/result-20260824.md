# SPIRE-SIES cross-corpus result — 2026-08-24

> **Read [`result-20260825.md`](result-20260825.md) alongside this document.** The
> two reference arms this file lists as missing (`zero-shot`, `sft-epoch1`) were
> subsequently run. They show the pipeline improves overall WER by 5.10 pp and
> worst-20%-speaker WER by 23.87 pp out-of-domain, which reframes the null result
> below: the equity gains are real and they transfer, but they are not produced by
> the `beta` fairness term. The findings in this document stand as written; they
> are about the penalty, not about the pipeline.


**Headline:** on a second corpus with real, speaker-disjoint identities, the
`reference_kl_beta=0.04` penalty shows **no benefit**. The seed whose in-domain
worst group improved is the seed that looks worse here; the other seed is a tight
null. Neither interval excludes zero, so the honest statement is *no demonstrated
cross-corpus efficacy*, with the seed-2026 evidence leaning toward harm.

Classification: cross-corpus generalization evidence. The **grouping unit is a
genuine corpus speaker**, so speaker-level statements here are defensible in a way
no Svarah number is. See "What this does not license" for the limits.

Registered contract: [`protocol.md`](protocol.md). Arm coordinates:
[`arms.md`](arms.md). Split audit: [`audit-20260820.md`](audit-20260820.md).

## Findings

### 1. No cross-corpus benefit; seed 2026 leans toward harm

Seed 2026 is worse on every measured endpoint, most sharply on the hardest
speakers (worst-20% speaker WER +4.56 pp), and its family gap more than
quadrupled (0.56 -> 2.33 pp). Its worst-family interval `[−1.17, +7.27]` is
wide and asymmetric with a long harm tail: occasionally better, sometimes much
worse. Seed 2027 is a tight null on every endpoint.

Neither interval excludes zero, so this is **not** a statistically established
harm. It is the absence of any benefit, plus a directional lean toward harm in
the one seed that looked best in-domain.

**Endpoint caution.** The Svarah in-domain endpoint is worst family *by acoustic
condition* (three families times three conditions). This is worst family on clean
speech over two families. They are different quantities and this document does
not subtract one from the other. What transfers is the *direction of effect*:
in-domain seed 2026 improved and seed 2027 worsened; out-of-domain seed 2026
worsened and seed 2027 did nothing. The one apparent success does not survive a
change of corpus.

### 2. The worst-family endpoint is fragile on this corpus

Family gaps are 0.56-2.33 pp, and in the seed-2027 pair the worst-family label
flips from Indo-Aryan to Dravidian
(`worst_family_label_changed_between_arms: true`). When two groups are nearly
tied, "worst-group WER" is close to a coin flip between them, so a worst-family
delta is a high-variance estimator of nothing very stable. The wide intervals
above are consistent with that. Any future protocol using a worst-group endpoint
should first check that the groups are actually separated.

### 3. On real speakers, family is close to the least informative axis

Consistently across all four arms:

| Axis | Spread |
| --- | ---: |
| language family | 0.6-2.3 pp |
| gender (Male always worst) | 6.0-7.7 pp |
| worst-20% speakers vs overall | ~36-41 pp |

Speaker-level variation dwarfs language-family variation by more than an order of
magnitude, and gender separates groups several times more than family does. The
FR-CISPO objective optimizes worst *family*.

This does **not** contradict Svarah, where family gaps were large (41 pp
zero-shot, 14 pp after SFT). It says family-gap prominence is **corpus
dependent**, and that when identities are real speakers rather than demographic
profile clusters, the dominant disparity is between speakers. That is a concrete
argument for choosing the grouping unit from evidence rather than convention.

## What this does not license

1. **No claim that the penalty is harmful.** Both worst-family intervals include
   zero. The defensible claim is no demonstrated benefit.
2. **No overall in-domain-versus-out-of-domain transfer number**, because the two
   endpoints differ (see Finding 1).
3. **No statement about whether the RL post-training helped at all
   out-of-domain.** Only the four matched-pair arms were run, so there is no
   zero-shot or SFT reference on this corpus. Both arms of each pair are
   FR-CISPO checkpoints. Adding `zero-shot` and `sft-epoch1` would close this.
4. **No robustness claim.** Clean speech only; no noise conditions were run here.
5. **The checkpoints themselves remain profile-cluster provenance.** The
   *evaluation* grouping is a real speaker, but these adapters were trained and
   selected under Svarah's 115 demographic profile clusters. Conclusions about the
   *method* still inherit that limitation, even though conclusions about *these
   checkpoints' behaviour on SPIRE speakers* do not.
6. **Two families, no Sino-Tibetan, no age axis**, and only 13 of 17 shipped
   languages appear in the validation split (see the audit).
