# Racial Disparities in Automated Speech Recognition

- **Authors/year:** Allison Koenecke, Andrew Nam, Emily Lake, Joe Nudell, Minnie Quartey, Zion Mengesha, Connor Toups, John R. Rickford, Dan Jurafsky, Sharad Goel (2020).
- **Stable record:** [DOI:10.1073/pnas.1915768117](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1915768117); [PubMed Central full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7149386/).
- **Verification/BibTeX:** PNAS publisher/DOI plus PubMed Central archival record; BibTeX fetched from DOI content negotiation.
- **Verified claim:** across five commercial ASR systems and matched speech snippets, average WER was 0.35 for Black speakers and 0.19 for white speakers. The article attributes the documented gap primarily to acoustic-model performance in its setting.
- **Method/results and reported metrics/datasets:** matched observational audit of 19.8 hours of speech with WER, not a training intervention.
- **Relevance:** establishes that aggregate WER can conceal consequential group disparities and that WER is the relevant observable.
